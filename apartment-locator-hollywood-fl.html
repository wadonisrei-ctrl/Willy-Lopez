Why an Apartment Locator Makes Sense in This Market

The stretch of Broward County running from Hollywood down through Dania Beach and Hallandale Beach has seen a lot of new apartment construction, with a wave of buildings opening between 2020 and 2026. New supply means buildings compete for renters, and the way they compete is with concessions: a month free, two months free, waived application and administrative fees, or a gift card at move-in.

That sounds great until you actually try to shop it. Concession offers change without notice, they usually are not reflected in the rent you see advertised on listing sites, and the terms vary in ways that matter. One building might offer two months free but require a 13 or 15 month lease. Another might advertise a lower base rent with no concession at all and actually cost you less over a year. The listing sites are not built to show you that difference.

That is the specific problem I solve. I track what is running across the buildings in this area, and when you tell me your budget and move-in date, I tell you which ones fit and what each one really costs per month after the concession is accounted for.

Pricing varies a lot depending on how new the building is and how close you are to the water. Here is the honest range across the buildings I track, so you can calibrate before you start looking.

How Concessions Actually Change What You Pay

This is the part most renters miss, and it is worth understanding even if you never contact me.

When a building offers two months free on a twelve month lease, you are not paying the advertised rent. You are paying twelve months of rent minus two, spread across twelve months. On a unit advertised at $2,600 a month, two months free works out to roughly $2,167 a month over the lease. That is about seventeen percent off, and it means that $2,600 unit may genuinely cost you less than a unit down the street advertised at $2,300 with no concession.

The lease term is what determines whether the deal is as good as it sounds. Two months free on a fifteen month lease is a smaller monthly discount than two months free on a twelve month lease, because the same free rent gets divided across more months. Some offers also cap the amount of free rent, or apply only to specific floor plans, or expire on a fixed date.

When I send you options, I run that math on each one so you are comparing real monthly costs instead of advertised numbers. It is a small thing that regularly changes which apartment is actually the best deal.
