Apartment Move-In Specials in Hollywood, FL

The Kinds of Specials You Will Actually See

Concessions show up in a few standard forms around Hollywood and the surrounding Broward cities, and they are not equally valuable.

Free rent is the headline version. One month free, two months free, sometimes a number of weeks instead of whole months. This is usually the biggest dollar value on the table.

Waived fees are smaller but real. Application fees and administrative fees are the common ones. These are worth a few hundred dollars in most cases, and unlike free rent they land immediately rather than being spread out.

Reduced deposits, or deposit alternatives that replace a traditional deposit with a smaller non-refundable fee. Useful for cash flow at move-in, but read carefully, because a non-refundable fee is money you never get back, while a deposit is money you can get back.

Look-and-lease bonuses, which require you to apply within a short window after touring, often 24 or 48 hours. These create real urgency, and that is the point of them.

Gift cards or move-in credits, which are straightforward but usually the smallest item.

The important thing is that these stack differently. Two months free plus a waived application fee is a very different offer from a gift card, even though both get advertised as a special.

How to Turn Any Special Into a Real Monthly Number

This is the calculation that matters, and it takes about ten seconds once you know it.

Take the total rent you will actually pay across the whole lease, then divide by the number of months you get to live there. That is your effective rent. It is the only number worth comparing between buildings.

Using round numbers to keep it clear. Say a one-bedroom is advertised at $2,000 a month with two months free on a twelve month lease. You pay ten months of rent, which is $20,000, across twelve months of living there. That is about $1,667 a month. Roughly seventeen percent below the advertised price.

Now the comparison that matters. A hypothetical building down the road advertises $1,800 with no special at all. It looks cheaper. It is not. You would pay $1,800 every month, against $1,667 at the first building. The listing with the higher sticker price is the cheaper apartment, and nothing on a listings site will tell you that.

The Lease Term Is the Part People Miss

The same free rent spread over a longer lease is worth less per month. This sounds obvious and it still catches people constantly, because the headline number does not change.

Two months free on a twelve month lease and two months free on a fifteen month lease are advertised identically. They are not the same offer. In the first case the discount is divided across twelve months. In the second it is divided across fifteen. Same free rent, noticeably smaller monthly benefit, and you are committed for three extra months.

Longer terms are not automatically bad. If you are settled and want to lock in a rate, a fifteen or sixteen month lease with a strong concession can be genuinely good, and it pushes your renewal past peak season, which can work in your favour. But you should be choosing that deliberately rather than being surprised by it.

So when a leasing office quotes you a special, always ask what lease term it requires. If they offer several terms, ask for the effective rent on each. Some will run it for you. If they will not, I will.

The Renewal Jump Nobody Warns You About

This is the part almost no one explains, and it is the most expensive surprise in the whole process.

Your concession applies to your first lease. It does not carry forward. When renewal comes around, the building generally quotes from the standard rent, not from the discounted number you have been paying.

Using the same round numbers as before. If you signed at an advertised $2,000 with two months free, you have been paying about $1,667. At renewal the building may quote you $2,000 or higher, even with no increase in their actual asking rent. To you it feels like a twenty percent rent hike. On their side, nothing changed. You simply stopped getting a concession that was always temporary.

This is worth planning for from the day you sign. Budget against the gross rent, not the effective rent, so the renewal is not a shock. Ask at signing what renewals have typically looked like at that community. And know that renewal is a negotiation, particularly if the building still has vacancy, because keeping a paying resident is cheaper for them than turning a unit over.

The other clause worth reading is concession recapture. Many leases state that if you break the lease early, you repay some or all of the concession you received. That can turn a cheap-looking early exit into a large bill, so if there is any chance you will need to leave early, find out before signing rather than after.

Fine Print That Changes the Value

A few things quietly shrink a special, and they are all worth asking about directly.

Caps on free rent. Some offers advertise months free but cap the dollar amount, so a larger or pricier unit does not get the full benefit.

Floor plan restrictions. Specials frequently apply only to specific units, often the ones that have been sitting empty. The apartment you actually want may not qualify.

Move-in date windows. Many offers require you to take occupancy by a certain date. If your timeline slips, the special can disappear.

How the free rent is applied. Sometimes it is a genuine free month with no rent due. Sometimes it is amortized, meaning the discount is spread evenly across every payment. Amortized is easier to budget for, but it is also the version most likely to come with a recapture clause.

Qualification requirements. Income multiples, credit thresholds and deposit terms can all differ for the discounted unit versus standard pricing.

None of this makes concessions a trap. They are genuinely valuable in this market and worth chasing. It just means the advertised headline is the start of the conversation, not the end of it.

Why This Market Runs So Many Specials

Concessions are common around Hollywood, Dania Beach and Hallandale Beach for a structural reason rather than a seasonal one.

A lot of newer apartment product has been delivered in this part of Broward in recent years. A building that has just opened has a large number of empty units and a strong incentive to fill them quickly, because an empty unit earns nothing while the building still carries its costs. Offering free rent to fill units faster is usually cheaper for them than holding out for full price and staying half empty.

That means the best concessions tend to cluster in newer buildings and in buildings that have recently had a wave of move-outs. It also means offers move quickly. A special that was running last month may be gone, extended, or replaced with a different structure entirely.
