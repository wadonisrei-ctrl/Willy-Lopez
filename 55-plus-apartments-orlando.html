Finding 55+ Housing in the Orlando Area

Most of the Orlando rental market is open-age housing. Dedicated 55+ communities are rarer, which means when a well-run one has availability, it tends to fill up fast, and buildings with a track record of good management and maintenance get first pick of applicants.

I focus on senior housing across the Orlando area, with my strongest relationships in Winter Park. At least one tenant in the household typically needs to be 55 or older to qualify, and I steer clients toward professionally managed buildings with responsive maintenance and on-site staff rather than smaller, less established operations.

Winter Park itself is one of Orlando's most desirable addresses. Tree-lined streets, walkable to Park Avenue dining and shops, and a quieter pace than downtown Orlando. It is a natural landing spot for retirees relocating from the Northeast, Midwest, or South Florida.

What Senior Communities in This Area Actually Offer

Floor plans vary by building, but expect one-bedroom and two-bedroom layouts sized for someone living alone or as a couple, occasionally with a small den. Square footage and layout are the biggest differences between floor plans in the same building, more than any other single factor.

Income requirements at senior communities are often more forgiving than standard apartments. Many use a lower multiple of monthly rent than the 2.5x to 3x used elsewhere in the market, which matters if you are living on Social Security or a fixed retirement distribution. I confirm the exact requirement for any specific community before you apply.

Pet policies vary too. Most senior communities I work with welcome cats and dogs with standard weight and breed limits, plus a combination of a one-time pet fee and monthly pet rent that differs building to building.

Rents at senior communities move with the season and with how full a building is running, the same as anywhere else in the market, so I do not publish numbers here that could be a month out of date by the time you read them. Contact me directly for current availability and pricing, and I will connect you with the leasing team the right way once we have talked through your situation.

Why Retirees Move to the Orlando Area

Florida is the top destination for retirees in the country, and Central Florida specifically has a few advantages over the more saturated South Florida market.

No state income tax. Florida has no personal income tax, which makes retirement income go further.

Lower cost of living than South Florida. Rents in the Orlando area generally run below Miami or Fort Lauderdale for comparable units, though the gap varies building to building.

Warm weather year-round. The Central Florida climate is milder than the coast, without the constant humidity and hurricane exposure of Southeast Florida.

Proximity to family. Many retirees follow adult children who moved to the Orlando metro for work in healthcare, tech, or the hospitality industry.

Season flexibility. You can choose to be here year-round or use a rental as a seasonal base and return north in summer.
