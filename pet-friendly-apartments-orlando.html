Why Renting With a Pet Is Harder Than It Looks

Most apartment websites say "pet-friendly" somewhere on the page. What they usually do not say up front is what that actually means at that specific community. One place caps dogs at 25 pounds.

Another allows large dogs but bans a list of breeds. A third is fine with your dog but charges a one-time fee plus monthly pet rent that changes the real cost of the lease.

Renters usually find this out late. You tour a place, fall in love with it, start the application, and then the leasing office mentions your dog's breed is on their restricted list. Now you are back to square one, sometimes with a non-refundable application fee already spent.

This is one of the situations where a locator genuinely earns their keep. Because I work with these communities directly, I ask about your specific pet before anything else, then I only send you places where your pet is actually approvable. No wasted applications, no surprise at the leasing desk.

I do not publish starting prices on this page, since they shift with occupancy and season across so many different areas. Tell me your budget, your move-in date, and where your dog or cat needs to fit, and I will tell you honestly which of these areas has something that works.

Reading an Orlando Pet Policy

Pet policies at Central Florida communities usually have three parts: a one-time pet fee or deposit, monthly pet rent per animal, and restrictions on breed, weight, or number of pets. The exact numbers vary from community to community and change over time, which is exactly why I do not publish specific fees here. A number that was right last month can be wrong today.

What I do instead: when you tell me about your pet, I call or email the leasing office and confirm the current policy in writing before you apply. Breed, weight, how many pets, service or support animal status, all of it. You see the real numbers for your shortlist before you spend anything on an application.

If you have a breed that commonly shows up on restricted lists, tell me early. Some of my communities are stricter than others, and knowing that up front lets me build your shortlist around places where your dog is genuinely welcome rather than technically allowed with an asterisk.

Service Animals and Emotional Support Animals

One thing many renters do not know: under the federal Fair Housing Act, housing providers must make reasonable accommodations for a qualifying service animal or emotional support animal, and pet fees and pet rent typically do not apply to them, even at communities with strict pet policies. HUD's enforcement standard narrowed in September 2025 to focus on animals trained to provide disability-related assistance, so confirm current guidance before assuming an older notice still applies.

If you have a service animal or an ESA with proper documentation, mention it when we talk. The application works a little differently, and I will make sure the community handles it as an accommodation request rather than a standard pet application. I am an apartment locator, not a lawyer, so for anything beyond the basics of the process I will point you to the actual HUD guidance rather than guess.

How the Process Works

Tell me your budget, move-in date, bedroom count, the area you want, and everything about your pet: type, breed, weight, how many. That last part is not small talk. It determines which communities go on your list at all.

I check your criteria against the communities I work with, confirm current availability and the exact pet policy with the leasing offices, and send you a short list of real options, usually within 24 hours. From there we set up a time for a video walkthrough or an in-person tour of your top pick. Once you are ready, you apply directly with the community and sign your lease with them.

The whole thing works remotely if you are moving from out of state. And it costs you nothing. The community pays my referral fee when you sign, and you pay the same rent you would pay walking in off the street. The only difference is you skip the part where the leasing office surprises you about your dog.
