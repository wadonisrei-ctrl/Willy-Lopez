Guide

What I Handle for a Landlord

You have a rental in Marion County and you want a good tenant in it. That is the whole job: I market the unit, screen who answers, and hand you someone worth signing with. Send me the address and your number and I will call you back within 24 business hours to walk through condition, rent, and how fast it can be on the market.

I am not a property management company. I do not collect your rent every month, I do not field the 2am maintenance call, and I am not going to pretend that service exists here. What I do is the part that actually decides whether a rental works out: who ends up living in your property.

A bad tenant costs you more than a vacant month ever will. Late rent, property damage, an eviction that drags on for months. Screening is where that risk gets caught or missed, and it is the part of this business I take the most seriously.

My fee is one month's rent, and you owe it only when a lease gets signed. If I do not place a tenant, you do not pay me anything. That is the whole arrangement.

How This Works, Step by Step

You tell me about the unit and your number. Address, bedrooms, condition, what you want for rent, and when you need someone in. I ask enough questions upfront that I am not calling you back three times for details I should have gotten the first time.

I market it and screen applicants. I write the listing, put it in front of renters actively looking in Marion County, field the calls and questions, and run the applications that come in. Screening means income verification, rental history, and a background check on anyone I bring to you as a serious applicant. You are not the one fielding calls from people who were never going to qualify.

You sign the lease and pay one month's rent. Once you have a tenant you are comfortable with, you sign the lease directly with them. My fee is due at that point, not before. I am not asking you to pay for effort, I am asking you to pay for a result.

Should You Rent It Out or Sell It?

Not every vacant property should go back on the rental market, and I would rather tell you that upfront than collect a placement fee on a property you should have sold.

If you inherited a house, if you are managing a property from out of state, or if the numbers on renting it barely clear your mortgage and taxes once you account for vacancy and repairs, selling can be the better move. A vacant rental sitting on the market for weeks while you carry the mortgage is a real cost, and so is a property that keeps needing money you are not getting back in rent.

On the other hand, plenty of owners are sitting on a property that rents well and does not need to be sold at all. It depends on your numbers, your timeline, and what you actually want out of the property long term, not on a generic rule.

I will not push you toward whichever option pays me. If renting it makes sense, I place a tenant. If selling makes more sense for your situation, I am also a licensed listing agent and can walk you through that side too. Either way, there is no cost to talk it through and no pressure to decide anything on the first call.

Where I Work

Tenant placement is Marion County: Ocala, Marion Oaks, and the surrounding area. I represent buyers and sellers directly here too, which means I already know the neighborhoods, what a given block actually rents for versus what an owner hopes it rents for, and what a serious applicant looks like in this market.

If your property sits outside Marion County, tell me anyway. I will give you a straight answer on whether I can help directly or point you to someone who can.
