The Memorial Campus Is Bigger Than One Building

Memorial Regional Hospital is the flagship of Memorial Healthcare System, one of the largest public healthcare systems in the country. Joe DiMaggio Children's Hospital sits on the same campus, and Memorial Regional Hospital South is part of the same system. Memorial Healthcare System reports more than 14,000 employees across the system.

What that means for your apartment search is simple. You are not really looking for an apartment in Hollywood. You are looking for an apartment relative to one specific campus, and those are not the same problem.

Someone with an ordinary office job can pick almost anywhere in the city and adjust their routine around it. You have a fixed address you will drive to hundreds of times a year, frequently on very little sleep. An apartment that looks fine on a map can sit on the wrong side of a corridor that backs up, or force a left turn across traffic that quietly costs you ten minutes in each direction, every single time.

I am not going to publish drive times on this page. I would be guessing, and you would be planning a relocation around my guess. Traffic on the roads feeding the campus varies by direction and by hour, and South Florida in February does not move like South Florida in August. What I will do is tell you which side of the campus a given option sits on so you can run it against your real report time in a map app before you commit. That takes ninety seconds and it beats any number I could invent here.

One more thing worth knowing at the start. The campus itself is not where the apartments are. Housing around a major medical center tends to sit in the neighborhoods surrounding it rather than on top of it, which is why "how close can I get" is usually the wrong question and "how easy is the drive" is the right one.

What Nights and Rotating Shifts Change About Picking an Apartment

This is the part nobody writing about Hollywood apartments bothers to cover, and it is the part that decides if you still like the place in six months.

If your rotation puts you on nights, your apartment has to work as a bedroom in broad daylight. That turns the normal priority list upside down.

Landscaping and trash pickup are the two biggest offenders. Most communities run crews on a set weekday schedule, usually starting early. A leaf blower outside the bedroom window at eight in the morning is a non-issue for a renter who left at seven thirty. It is a real problem for someone who got home at eight fifteen. Ask the leasing office which days the crews come through and where the compactor or valet trash staging area sits relative to the unit you are considering.

Sun exposure matters more than people expect. An east-facing bedroom takes the full morning. Blackout curtains help, but they do not fix a room that soaks up heat all afternoon while the air conditioning fights back, and they do not fix a west-facing sliding door glowing at six in the evening when you are trying to get four more hours in.

Then there is the amenity deck. A pool, a dog run or a playground directly below the bedroom is a selling point for a day-shift renter and a nightly annoyance for you. Ask which floor plans back onto the amenity area, and quietly rule them out.

Coming home at the end of a night shift also changes what you want from parking and building access. Covered or gated parking, a short walk from the space to your door, lighting that actually works at three in the morning, and package storage you can reach without a leasing agent on site. If you sleep through every delivery window, secure package lockers are worth real money to you and worth nothing to somebody who works from home.

None of this appears on a listing site. All of it is answerable in one phone call to a leasing office, and asking those questions is a good part of what I do before anything reaches your inbox.

Contract Length Is the Real Problem for Travel Nurses

Travel contracts commonly run thirteen weeks, sometimes with an extension dangled near the end. Standard apartment leases in this market run twelve months. Those two numbers do not fit inside each other, and pretending otherwise is how people end up paying to escape a lease.

There are three honest paths, and the right one depends on how likely you are to extend or convert to staff.

The first is a conventional twelve month lease. This makes sense if you have a genuine shot at extending, if you are hoping to convert to a permanent role, or if you already know South Florida is where you want to land. You get normal pricing, normal apartment quality, and access to whatever move-in concession is running. All of the risk sits in the exit.

The second is short-term furnished housing, booked through a corporate housing provider or a private landlord rather than a conventional community. You pay a meaningful premium per month for that flexibility. For a single thirteen week assignment with no expectation of extension, the premium is often still cheaper than breaking a year lease.

The third is your agency's placed housing, if they offer housing instead of a stipend. Less control, less hassle, and worth pricing against the other two rather than accepting by default.

Where I can genuinely help is the first path, and there is a clause you need to understand before you sign one.

If a community gives you free months as a move-in concession, the lease very likely contains a concession recapture provision. That means if you leave early, you repay the discount you already received, on top of whatever early termination fee applies. Using round numbers to show the shape of it: sign at an advertised $2,000 a month with two months free, and you have received $4,000 in concession value. Walk at month five because your contract ended and was not renewed, and that $4,000 can come back as a bill sitting next to the termination fee. The concession that made the apartment attractive is exactly what makes leaving it expensive.

So here are the questions to ask before signing, in order. What is the early termination fee, and is it a flat amount or a rent-through-reletting arrangement? Is there a concession recapture clause? Does the community offer any lease term shorter than twelve months at all? Some do. It usually costs more per month, and for someone on assignment that can still be the cheaper decision overall.

Locking Something In Before Your Start Date

Most healthcare workers who reach out to me are not in Hollywood yet. They have an offer letter or a signed contract, a start date a few weeks out, and no realistic way to fly down and spend a weekend touring.

That is solvable, and it is most of what I actually do.

Here is the honest version of how it works. I am based in Ocala, roughly 250 miles north of Hollywood. I am not going to meet you at a property or walk a unit with you, and I would rather say that plainly now than have you discover it on the day. What I do instead is line up the options, confirm what is available and what the terms actually are with the leasing office, and set you up to see the place without me. Most communities in this part of Broward run self-guided tours, and many will do a live video walkthrough over a phone call so you can ask about closet space, water pressure and the view out the bedroom window while someone is standing in the room.

Timing is what people get wrong. Applications take time to process, and a unit that is available today may be gone in three weeks. Working backward from your start date is far more useful than working forward from today. If your first shift is the fifteenth, you want keys before the fifteenth, which means an approved application well before that, which means starting earlier than feels necessary.

Have your paperwork ready before you apply rather than scrambling afterward. For a permanent hire that usually means the offer letter with the salary stated on it. For a contract role it means the contract itself or the agency's pay packet breakdown. Leasing offices verify income, and a document that states the number plainly moves faster than a verbal figure or a screenshot.

One last piece of timing catches a lot of people. Contract start dates and lease start dates rarely line up neatly. If your assignment begins on the first and the only unit you like comes available on the eighth, that is a week in a hotel. Price that gap into the decision now instead of discovering it later.

Why There Is No Rent Number on This Page

I get asked for a figure constantly and I deliberately do not post one.

Rents around Hollywood move, concessions appear and vanish without notice, and the same floor plan can price differently in March than it did in January. Any number I published here would be wrong within weeks, and you would be planning a relocation around something I wrote once and forgot to update. You deserve what is true today, so ask me and I will tell you.

What I can explain is how to think about the budget, because that part does not go stale.

If you are taking a permanent role, the budget conversation is the ordinary one. Communities screen income against the rent, usually as a multiple of gross monthly income, and each property sets its own threshold rather than following a legal standard. I check the exact requirement for anything I send you.

If you are on assignment with a housing stipend, the conversation changes in a way most guides skip entirely. A stipend is a fixed dollar amount, so your ceiling is a true ceiling rather than a soft guideline. And how a leasing office counts stipend income is not standardized. Some treat the full pay packet as income. Others look only at taxable wages and disregard the untaxed portion, which can make a well-paid contract nurse look under-qualified on paper. Ask that question before you pay an application fee, because the answer decides if you need a guarantor, a larger deposit, or nothing extra at all.

On scope I will be straight with you. Hollywood sits toward the pricier end of Broward, and a corridor anchored by a major hospital campus draws steady demand from people who need to be near it. That does not make it impossible for you. It makes it worth a real conversation before you get attached to a spot on the map. Tell me your budget and your must-haves and I will tell you straight whether I can help you here, or point you somewhere else in the county that fits better. Redirecting you early is more useful to you than burning three weeks of your relocation timeline being polite about it.
