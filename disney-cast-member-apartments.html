Why Cast Members Look at Davenport and Kissimmee

If you are starting a role at Disney, Universal, or one of the resort hotels, you do not need to live inside Orlando proper. Most cast members end up in Davenport or Kissimmee, both south and southwest of the parks, because rents are generally lower than closer-in Orlando neighborhoods and the commute is still manageable.

Davenport sits southwest of the Disney property and is one of the more affordable areas in the whole metro for newer construction. Kissimmee runs along US-192, the corridor most directly connected to the attractions area, and has a long history as a hospitality-worker neighborhood.

Both areas see a steady flow of new cast members, seasonal hires, and College Program participants moving in and out, so leasing offices in these corridors are used to shorter-notice move-ins and out-of-state applicants.

Rent in Davenport and Kissimmee for Cast Members

I do not publish rent figures on this page, and that is on purpose. Pricing at communities in this corridor moves week to week, move-in specials appear and disappear, and I would rather give you a confirmed number from a real conversation than a stale one from a web page.

What is worth understanding instead is what actually moves the price. Building age is the biggest factor: newer construction generally costs more but comes with in-unit laundry and modern layouts, and it is where the most aggressive specials tend to show up, since a building still filling its first units cares more about speed than top dollar. Lease length matters too. Cast members on a seasonal contract or a College Program timeline often need something shorter than a standard 12-month lease, and shorter terms can price differently than a full year.

Davenport tends to run below Kissimmee for comparable newer construction, and Kissimmee puts you closer to the US-192 attractions corridor, which matters if you are working at Universal or want a shorter drive on shift-change days. Tell me your budget, your contract length, and which park you are working at, and I will tell you honestly which of the two areas fits and what is realistically available right now.

How a Move-In Special Changes Your Monthly Cost

Move-in specials are common in both corridors: a chunk of free rent, waived application and administrative fees, or a bonus for applying quickly after touring. The part most renters miss is that a free-rent special is not a discount on one month, it is a discount spread across the whole lease.

Using round hypothetical numbers to keep the math clear: say a one-bedroom lists at sixteen hundred dollars a month and the community offers two months free on a twelve-month lease. You are paying ten months of rent across twelve months of living there, which lowers your real monthly cost by a meaningful amount depending on the exact terms.

That means a unit with a higher advertised rent and a strong concession can genuinely cost less per month than a cheaper-looking unit with no special running. I run that comparison on every option I send you, and I confirm what is actually still active with the leasing office before you apply, since advertised specials expire or change terms without notice.
